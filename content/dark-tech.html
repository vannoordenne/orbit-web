Dark Tech

Research line

How can deliberately exaggerating and materialising the dark side of emerging technologies foster critical discussion, ethical reflection and technological literacy?

In this research line we investigate the Dark Tech Method: what happens when we stop designing solutions and deliberately design the systems we fear.

Through speculative design and provocative prototyping, undesirable technological futures are exaggerated and made tangible. These provocatypes turn abstract concerns around power, manipulation, surveillance, dependency and other societal consequences into things that can be experienced, questioned and discussed.

Our research investigates how this process supports critical discussion and ethical reflection, and how the approach can be applied across educational, professional and public contexts.

The method has been developed through several years of practice-based research across education, applied research, workshops, prototypes and public experiments, including the Dark Tech Studio at Amsterdam University of Applied Sciences and the CoE-CI-supported Tinker: Ethical Techlab project.
